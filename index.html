About

My research has evolved across different areas of economics. Before my doctoral studies, I worked on economic history. During my PhD at Toulouse School of Economics, my research focused on public economics and the economics of harmful goods, including applications to health-related policy.

Since joining the Banco Central del Uruguay as a full-time researcher, my research has increasingly focused on questions relevant to monetary policy and financial stability in Uruguay. My current work combines macroeconomic models with administrative and micro-level data, with two main directions: heterogeneous-agent macroeconomics and the dynamics of earnings and household balance sheets; and empirical research on household and firm indebtedness and credit markets.
